NASA is enabling commercial industry to build, own, and operate space systems, while the agency purchases services to meet its science and research needs. Through public‑private partnerships, NASA is expanding access to space for more science, more people, and more opportunities.

On Nov. 15, 2020, NASA astronauts Mike Hopkins, Victor Glover, and Shannon Walker, along with JAXA astronaut Soichi Noguchi, launched from Kennedy Space Center to the International Space Station on the SpaceX Crew‑1 mission — the first operational flight under NASA’s Commercial Crew Program.

As the International Space Station nears the end of operations, NASA plans to transition to a new low Earth orbit model to continue leveraging microgravity benefits. Through commercial partnerships, NASA aims to maintain its leadership in microgravity research to support its exploration missions and ensure continued benefits for humanity.

NASA is returning America to the Moon through its Artemis missions. American innovation is leading the way, and the future at the Moon holds promise for a robust lunar marketplace. NASA’s strategy stimulates the commercial space industry, which drives new ideas, brings down costs, and grows the business opportunities that can foster a lunar economy and serve other customers for the benefit of humanity.

Private astronaut missions to the International Space Station help pave the way toward commercial space stations as part of NASA’s efforts to develop a thriving low Earth orbit ecosystem and marketplace.

NASA’s commercial providers, SpaceX and Blue Origin, are building the human landing systems that will carry Artemis astronauts to the lunar surface and back to lunar orbit for their ride home to Earth aboard the Orion spacecraft.

NASA is committed to maintaining a continuous human presence in low Earth orbit as the agency transitions from the International Space Station to commercial space stations.

NASA is working with several American companies to deliver science and technology to the lunar surface through the Commercial Lunar Payload Services initiative. These companies, ranging in size, bid on delivering payloads for NASA. Under Artemis, commercial deliveries are performing science experiments, testing technologies, and demonstrating capabilities to help NASA explore the Moon.

NASA’s Commercial Crew Program is delivering on its goal of safe, reliable, and cost-effective human transportation to and from the International Space Station from the United States through a partnership with American private industry.
